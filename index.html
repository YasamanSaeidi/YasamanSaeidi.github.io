About

I am a PhD candidate in Economics at the London School of Economics, where I work with Stephen Machin and Tom Kirchmaier on the economics of crime and labour markets.

My research asks how features of the built environment and the wider economy shape criminal behaviour and victimisation. My job market paper studies what happens to crime when a city builds new transport infrastructure, using recorded crime data from Greater Manchester Police alongside the Crime Survey of England and Wales. Related work examines the effect of the cost of living crisis on offending, and the link between alcohol, football, and domestic abuse.

I work with administrative and survey microdata, and use machine learning and satellite imagery where conventional data are unavailable — most recently on a World Bank project measuring economic growth and climate indicators in Vietnam. Before the PhD I read Electrical Engineering at Sharif University of Technology.

Job market paper

Job market paper

Transport Innovation and Crime

This study assesses how newly built transport infrastructure influences the temporal and spatial dynamics of crime, exploring underlying mechanisms. The research also examines variations in effects across different crime types, offenders' characteristics, and victimisation experience. Initially, the focus is on the Manchester Metrolink, a comprehensive tram system in Greater Manchester, England, using data from the Greater Manchester Police on recorded crimes. The study then extends to all areas in England and Wales with newly established tram networks, incorporating the Crime Survey of England and Wales to assess citizens' crime reporting and victimisation. This analysis offers critical insights into the relationship between transport innovation and crime, contributing to urban planning and policy decisions.
